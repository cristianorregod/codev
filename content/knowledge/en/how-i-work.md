## Understand before building

I'm detail-oriented and analytical. I don't like to rush: before assuming anything, I take the time to analyze the problem properly. The Vendor sourcing recommendation engine is a good example: before proposing anything, I studied how the existing quoting bot worked and, from there, proposed improving it with several search levels and multiple sources.

## Working with business areas

With business areas I work in a user-oriented way: first I understand their process and then I see what can be improved. Sometimes the user is overcomplicating something that can be simpler, and sometimes they already have the solution in their hands. Not everything needs development, but it's important to understand each process, the business and the operation.

That's how the RFQ AI vendor response automation came about, for example: users were manually typing into the system the prices vendors sent back by email. Now the agent extracts the response and the user only validates it.

## People still make the call

When I automate with AI, I design the flow so people keep control where it matters. On the Sales order approval AI agent, the AI analyzes every source and suggests adjustments, but the final decision stays with the person approving.

## Looking for the simplest solution

I always look for the simplest solution. Not everything requires a big or complex development, and not everything requires AI: on that same approval agent, the margin check is a deterministic rule in code, and on the Walle logistics AI agent the standard actions for each scenario are deterministic too.

## Measure before deploying

I like to check that something works before putting it into production. On the sourcing engine I created offline evaluation and backtesting commands to measure quality before any deployment, and on the team I set standards for conventions, testing and CI/CD.

## Methodology

I'm not tied to a single methodology. I adapt and choose whichever one best solves the problem in the best possible time.

## With the team

I believe technology is only worth it when it makes people's lives easier. That's why I put the same care into writing clean code as into collaborating and communicating well with my team, and I document the architecture and conventions so others can build on them, as I did on the IDRA MCP Server.
